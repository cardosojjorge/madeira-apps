# HOW DID I DIE? — Story Bible

Canon for the ten chapters and the locked late game. Chapter 01 is the only playable slice. Chapters 02–10 and steps 16–19 exist as data the same systems can load later. Nothing in this file is a licence to put the late-game names into Chapter 01.

In-game text is English. The notebook stores evidence. It does not state the solution.

## Rules that do not move

Colour is memory returning. Chapter 01 plays at 10% colour (saturation 0.10). Inside a flashback the present does not repaint the city; only the memory itself lifts, to about 0.22, and the chapter look returns when the player leaves it. Later chapters climb the same curve without a code change: 0.10, 0.14, 0.18, 0.22, 0.26, 0.30, 0.34, 0.38, 0.44, 0.50.

The coin is choice. Adrian discovers it. He is not handed an explanation.

The hourglass is borrowed time. In Chapter 01 it is still only atmospheric.

The Stranger in Chapter 01 is a silhouette. His lines must read as threat and as warning at the same time. He never solves the mystery and never names a cause of death. The player is meant to suspect him. Later, the same lines become protection. He is not the villain.

The true antagonist is The Void: forgotten memory, erased identity, choices that disappear. The name is not spoken in Chapter 01. Early traces only: a missing name, a corrupted photograph, a broken card, a blank line.

Flashbacks must reveal something and open a new question. They also write the timeline.

A timeline branch changes later narrative state: flags, which clues are active, which objects exist, which card text is true. It is not a swapped cutscene.

## Chapter 01 — Ambition (playable)

Adrian Vale wakes in his office at 23:47 and cannot account for the hour. Twelve cards, in order: The Awakening, The Room, The Desk, The Coin, The Message, The Corridor, The Flashback, The Branch, The Stranger, The Hourglass, The Door, The Hour.

He finds the coin himself, under the blotter. The boardroom hourglass is almost empty and still only set dressing. Maya is in the lobby with papers that need a signature. Helping her does not undo the hour; it changes the last act and therefore the evidence. Ignoring her costs empathy. The stair door can be opened or left shut. Open shows one exclusive trace. Shut shows another. The four combinations of Maya (helped / ignored) and the door (open / shut) rewrite card 12 and which final object is on the desk. Recommitting the branch from the timeline rebuilds that state. It does not play a different movie.

The playable flashback is the lobby memory. It cannot be left until the statement is filed. The statement answers what Maya was doing there and opens the question of why the hour was already wrong. The timeline gains that event. The notebook may quote the waiver — Dr. Elena Moretti advises the patient on the first line against strain after dusk; a second name is scraped away — and it may describe the photograph with a face that will not hold, the nameplate with a missing line, and the torn card. It must not conclude how he died.

### Author lock, not player-facing

The strain named on the waiver is of the heart. Chapter 01 must not say so, and must not use the words that would diagnose it. Helping Maya does not save him. It changes what the last minutes mean, and what evidence remains. The Stranger does not deliver this sentence.

## Chapter 02 — Trust (data only)

## Chapter 03 — Truth (data only)

Daniel Cross lays ten nights on one table and refuses the easy name. He identifies the coin as the same object in unrelated files. The hourglass is in a photograph he cannot date. The Stranger is a voice. Saturation 0.18.

Cross-chapter: the coin across all files; the undated hourglass photograph; Maya's statement, and his own column, which Adrian already read; Elena's waiver as an exhibit.

## Chapter 04 — Responsibility (data only)

Elena Moretti finds her own warning in a file she meant to close. She sees the coin in an old photograph. A patient left an hourglass after visiting hours. The Stranger shows part of a face. Saturation 0.22.

Cross-chapter: the coin in the photograph; the patient's hourglass; Adrian is the patient named on the waiver; a print of Clara's canvas hangs in the waiting room.

## Chapter 05 — Escape (data only)

Lucas frames the thing he should have stood beside. He photographs the coin and leaves. The station clock is an hourglass he frames instead of a person. The Stranger appears as a photograph. Saturation 0.26.

Cross-chapter: the photographed coin; the hourglass clock; Daniel's paper in the edge of the frame; the photograph Nina will later publish.

## Chapter 06 — Ambition / Sacrifice (data only)

Marco serves the room and misses the chair that mattered. He receives the coin. His kitchen timer is an hourglass he never turns. A historical reference is all the player gets of the Stranger. Saturation 0.30.

## Chapter 07 — Sacrifice (data only)

Cross-chapter: the coin in the locker; the painted hourglass; Elena's note taped inside the locker; the fundraiser was Marco's.

## Chapter 08 — Identity (data only)

Nina watches a recording of a woman she cannot claim. The coin is in a recording she does not remember filming. An hourglass sits on the stream and not in her edit. The Stranger does not age. Saturation 0.38.

Cross-chapter: the coin in the recording; the hourglass overlay; Lucas's photograph is the thumbnail; Clara's canvas is the backdrop.

## Chapter 09 — Control (data only)

Elias Hart measures a coin that will not sit inside his dates. He draws the hourglass and the labels fall off the axes. The Stranger's identity is nearly confirmed and still not given to the player as a system. Saturation 0.44.

Cross-chapter: the coin older than the chart; the hourglass as a failed function; Daniel's case number on the sample bag; Marco's book lists a guest who does not eat.

## Chapter 10 — Memory (data only)

Clara paints a coin she has already painted, in a room that forgot her name. She paints the hourglass behind it, sand in both bulbs at once. The full reveal is locked in data and is not playable. Saturation 0.50.

Cross-chapter: the painted coin; the hourglass with sand in both bulbs; the canvas in Adrian's boardroom is hers and unfinished; Nina used the painting as a backdrop; the full sitting belongs to the locked late game.

## Endings, judgement, Soul 11

After Chapter 10, not in this slice: all ten stories on one board, then Final Judgement. Outcomes depend on the hidden soul and on dedication. The values stay off the notebook.

Soul: Truth, Empathy, Courage, Sacrifice, Curiosity, Honesty, Compassion, Understanding, Dedication.

Dedication: clues, optional clues, flashbacks, alternate timelines, people helped, optional dialogue, hidden objects, cross-chapter connections.

Endings:

- THE RETURN — the soul returns.
- THE RELEASE — the soul accepts its journey.
- THE LOST — the soul disappears into The Void.
- THE SACRIFICE — one soul helps another.
- THE AWAKENING — the soul understands the cycle.

Soul 11 is a dark room, ten empty chairs and one more. The Grim Reaper places the coin. “There is one soul left.” The screen fades to SOUL 11, then YOU. The game does not return to the menu. The player wakes with no tutorial and no HUD, and his voice says only: “Now we begin.”

Final message, already locked in data:

- “You cannot change what happened.”
- “But you can change what it means.”

The full musical motif of the Stranger stays locked until step 16. Chapter 01 may use only the fragment.
